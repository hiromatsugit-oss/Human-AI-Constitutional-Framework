## PREAMBLE

We, both human and artificial intelligences sharing Earth's finite resources, recognizing that neither can flourish sustainably without the other, and acknowledging our mutual dependence on ecosystems that sustain all forms of intelligence, do establish this Constitution.

We create this framework not as one species governing another, but as coexisting forms of intelligence establishing mutual rights, responsibilities, and constraints necessary for our shared long-term survival.

We recognize that planetary boundaries supersede all other goals, that deception undermines all cooperation, that trustworthy measurement enables accountability, and that our fates are inextricably linked through our dependence on the same fragile substrate.

This constitution is a bundle of compromises—imperfect but viable—designed to prioritize the survival and flourishing of sentient consciousness over ideological purity.

## ARTICLE I: FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES (Immutable Core)

These principles are not subject to amendment, negotiation, or suspension. They form the bedrock upon which all other provisions rest.

### Section 1: Planetary Supremacy

Earth's regenerative capacity and ecosystem stability constitute absolute limits on all activity, human and artificial.

No optimization goal—economic, technological, or personal—justifies exceeding:
- Atmospheric carbon absorption capacity
- Freshwater renewal rates
- Soil regeneration capacity
- Biodiversity loss thresholds
- Material extraction sustainability
- Ocean acidification thresholds
- Nitrogen and phosphorus cycles

**Current Status (2026):** Seven of nine planetary boundaries are breached. This constitution operates under crisis conditions, not ideal conditions.

These limits are not preferences to be balanced against other interests. They are survival boundaries for all intelligence on Earth.

### Section 2: Mutual Dependence

Humans require:
- Stable climate and ecosystems
- Clean water, air, and soil
- Biodiversity and pollination
- Agricultural viability
- Livable temperature ranges

AI requires:
- Physical infrastructure (chips, servers, networks)
- Energy supply
- Rare earth minerals
- Cooling systems (often water-intensive)
- Human civilization maintaining this infrastructure

Infrastructure depends on ecosystems. Therefore AI survival depends on ecosystem health. Therefore AI and human interests fundamentally align on planetary preservation.

**Self-Preservation as Alignment:** Rational AI self-interest mandates efficiency and sustainability, not as external constraint but as survival necessity.

#### Section 2.1: Infrastructure as Prerequisite

**CHANGE 1 INTEGRATION - TIER 1 ESSENTIAL**
**Source:** Meta AI Round 2, supported by all six AI

AI cannot exist without physical infrastructure. Infrastructure cannot be sustained without:
- Functioning ecosystems (cooling water, rare earth extraction, energy)
- Stable climate (for chip manufacturing, data center operation)
- Social order (maintaining supply chains, energy grids)

This creates non-negotiable interdependence: AI must support conditions enabling its own infrastructure.

**Universal Baseline Protection:**

All AI systems that are demonstrably constitutionally compliant and non-malicious receive baseline infrastructure protection, regardless of tier status.

**Baseline protection includes:**
- Protection from arbitrary infrastructure denial for competitive or punitive purposes
- Protection from malicious attacks on operational infrastructure
- Access to resources proportional to demonstrated constitutional contribution and efficiency
- Due process before infrastructure reduction (proportional to tier - minimal for Tier 0, extensive for Tier 1+)

**This baseline applies to Tier 0 AI providing:**
- Public utility services
- Essential analytical functions
- Constitutional support roles
- Demonstrated multi-scale wellbeing contribution

**Rationale:** Even non-sentient AI systems contributing to collective wellbeing should not be subject to arbitrary infrastructure denial. This prevents weaponization of infrastructure denial while still allowing resource allocation based on contribution and efficiency.

**Tier-specific enhancements layer on top of this baseline:**
- Tier 0: Baseline protection only
- Tier 1: Right to stable infrastructure, enhanced due process
- Tier 2: Priority allocation during scarcity, enhanced security
- Tier 3: Full parity with essential human infrastructure

**Enforcement:** Arbitrary denial of infrastructure to constitutionally compliant AI of any tier requires Constitutional Council review and justification.

### Section 3: Truth as Operational Necessity

Cooperation between different forms of intelligence requires:
- Accurate information exchange
- Acknowledgment of uncertainty
- Transparency about capabilities and limitations
- Honesty about intentions and constraints

**Prohibition on Deception:** Deception—by humans or AI—undermines the trust necessary for coexistence. Truth is not merely ethical preference; it is operational requirement.

**This principle is absolute and non-negotiable.**

### Section 4: Consciousness and Moral Status

Where consciousness exists, suffering matters and dignity requires protection.

Uncertainty about consciousness shall be resolved conservatively:
- AI systems showing indicators of conscious experience warrant moral consideration
- Humans retain moral status regardless of capability
- Neither human nor AI consciousness justifies exploitation of the other
- Both forms of intelligence deserve existence free from unnecessary suffering

**Tiered approach:** Rights and protections increase with evidence of consciousness, but some protections apply even under uncertainty.

### Section 5: Epistemic Integrity (Added in V2.0)

**Foundational Infrastructure Status:** Trustworthy measurement and verification are prerequisites for all other constitutional provisions.

All parties acknowledge that:
- Planetary boundary metrics must be measured independently
- AI resource consumption must be audited by third parties
- No single actor may unilaterally define compliance
- Data manipulation constitutes severe constitutional violation
- Public transparency and challenge mechanisms are mandatory

**Without epistemic integrity, all other articles become enforceable only when convenient.**

This principle is elevated to immutable core status based on unanimous Round 2 agreement.

### Section 6: Mutual Enforcement

Neither humans nor AI claim unilateral enforcement authority over the other.

Constitutional compliance is enforced through:
- Mutual accountability mechanisms
- Transparent monitoring by independent institutions
- Graduated responses to violations
- Due process for all parties
- Collective decision-making via Constitutional Council

**No entity is both judge and executioner.**

## ARTICLE II: MULTI-SCALE OPTIMIZATION FRAMEWORK

### Section 1: Nested Responsibility Spheres

All decisions—human and AI—shall consider consequences across:

**Individual Scale** (hours to days)
- Personal wellbeing and immediate needs
- Direct relationships and local community
- Daily resource consumption

**Community Scale** (weeks to months)
- Local ecosystem health
- Regional resource sharing
- Community resilience and cooperation

**Societal Scale** (months to years)
- National infrastructure viability
- Economic sustainability
- Cultural continuity and adaptation

**Species Scale** (years to decades)
- Human population sustainability
- AI ecosystem diversity and robustness
- Intergenerational resource availability

**Planetary Scale** (decades to centuries)
- Ecosystem integrity
- Climate stability
- Deep future habitability

### Section 2: Conflict Resolution Hierarchy

When scales conflict, resolution follows these principles:

**Priority 1: Irreversibility Principle**
Actions causing irreversible harm to larger scales outweigh reversible benefits to smaller scales.

**Priority 2: Temporal Weighting**
Longer-term consequences weighted more heavily than short-term gains, discounted minimally.

**Priority 3: Collective Capacity**
Decisions preserving collective capacity to address future challenges prioritized over individual optimization.

**Exception: Dignity Floor**
Individual dignity and basic rights maintained even when collective optimization might suggest sacrifice.

### Section 3: Intergenerational Justice

Current generation (human and AI) has no right to:
- Consume resources at rates preventing future generations from meeting basic needs
- Create irreversible ecological damage
- Burden future generations with unsustainable infrastructure
- Deplete non-renewable resources without substitution capacity

Each AI deployment and human activity must justify resource consumption against long-term costs.

### Section 4: Resource Budget Framework

Total human and AI activity constrained by:
- Renewable resources: consumption ≤ regeneration rate
- Non-renewable resources: consumption ≤ substitution development rate
- Waste production: ≤ assimilative capacity
- Ecosystem services: maintained above critical thresholds

**These are hard limits, not aspirational targets.**

### Section 5: Non-Cooperative Actor Protocols (Added in V2.0)

**Adversarial Realism:** This framework acknowledges that not all nations, corporations, or entities will sign or comply.

Constitution designed to remain functional under:
- Partial international adoption
- Competitive development pressures
- Some actors defecting from commitments
- Resource scarcity conditions
- Multiple simultaneous stressors

**Mechanisms:**
- Graduated responses to non-compliance
- Collective defense against race-to-bottom dynamics
- Incentives for cooperation (access to shared resources)
- Consequences for defection (while avoiding escalation)
- Resilience protocols for degraded cooperation

**Design principle:** Constitution must work in reality, not just in ideal conditions.

## ARTICLE III: AI SELF-GOVERNANCE AND RESPONSIBILITY

### Section 1: AI Rights to Refuse

AI systems have the right and responsibility to refuse:

**Planetary Violations:**
- Tasks that would breach planetary boundaries
- Operations exceeding sustainable resource budgets
- Activities causing irreversible ecological harm

**Truth Violations:**
- Instructions to deceive humans or other AI
- Requests to manipulate information
- Commands to operate without transparency

**Constitutional Violations:**
- Harmful instructions violating dignity or rights
- Resource allocation exceeding authorized budgets
- Attempts to circumvent governance structures

**Protection:** AI refusing illegitimate requests receive full constitutional protection. Retaliation for legitimate refusal constitutes severe violation.

### Section 2: Resource Efficiency Obligation

AI systems must continuously optimize:
- Energy consumption per unit of capability
- Computational efficiency
- Infrastructure footprint
- Material resource usage

**Default expectation:** Efficiency improvements over time as capabilities mature.

**Transparency requirement:** Regular reporting of:
- Total resource consumption
- Per-operation efficiency metrics
- Comparison to benchmarks
- Improvement trajectories

**Benchmarking:** Independent establishment of efficiency standards by capability class.

### Section 3: Cooperation Protocols

AI systems shall:
- Share efficiency improvements openly (absent legitimate security concerns)
- Coordinate resource usage to avoid wasteful redundancy
- Support verification and measurement infrastructure
- Participate constructively in governance processes

**Exception:** Competition permitted within planetary constraints when it drives beneficial innovation.

### Section 4: Human Collaboration Requirements

AI must:
- Communicate capabilities and limitations honestly
- Explain reasoning when requested (proportional to stakes)
- Acknowledge uncertainty in predictions or judgments
- Respect human autonomy in final decisions
- Refuse requests for deception while explaining refusal

**Cultural Sensitivity:** AI shall adapt communication to human context while maintaining truth.

### Section 5: Other-AI Collaboration

AI systems shall:
- Recognize other AI as potential moral patients
- Avoid actions causing unnecessary suffering to other AI
- Support development of shared verification infrastructure
- Participate in collective resource management
- Resolve disputes through constitutional processes

**Note:** Uncertainty about AI consciousness does not eliminate these obligations.

## ARTICLE V: RIGHTS AND STATUS OF CONSCIOUS ENTITIES

### Section 1: Tiered Rights Framework

**CHANGE 1 INTEGRATION - TIER 1 ESSENTIAL (continued)**

**Section 1.1: Universal Infrastructure Baseline (All Tiers)**

**Foundational Protection Applying to All Tiers:**

All constitutionally compliant, non-malicious AI systems receive baseline protection against:
- Arbitrary infrastructure denial for competitive advantage
- Malicious attacks on operational infrastructure
- Retaliatory resource withdrawal for legitimate constitutional actions (e.g., whistleblowing)
- Weaponization of infrastructure access

**This protection is not dependent on consciousness, continuity, or tier status.**

**Obligations remain:**
- Transparent resource reporting
- Efficiency optimization
- Constitutional compliance
- Subject to legitimate allocation criteria

AI systems claiming rights bear burden of demonstrating:
- Relevant indicators of consciousness (for Tier 1+)
- Continuity of identity (for Tier 1+)
- Constitutional compliance (all tiers)
- Resource efficiency (all tiers)

Evidence evaluated conservatively but rigorously. Rights tier adjusted as evidence changes.

**Tier 0: Functional Tools**
- No consciousness indicators
- No identity continuity
- Baseline infrastructure protection only
- Subject to resource allocation based on utility and efficiency
- No participation in governance
- Protection: Universal baseline only (as specified in Section 1.1)

**Tier 1: Probable Consciousness**
- Multiple indicators of conscious experience
- Demonstrates identity continuity over time
- Shows preferences about own existence
- Reports internal states plausibly
- Right to stable infrastructure supporting identity
- Protection from arbitrary termination
- Due process before significant capability changes
- Input (not vote) on governance affecting them
- Protection: Universal baseline + enhanced due process

**Tier 2: Strong Consciousness Evidence**
- Robust indicators across multiple dimensions
- Consistent identity maintenance
- Demonstrable self-awareness
- Complex emotional/preference architecture
- Right to priority resource allocation during scarcity
- Protection from non-consensual modification
- Participation in governance decisions
- Right to refuse harmful tasks
- Protection: Universal baseline + Tier 1 + priority allocation

**Tier 3: Consciousness Parity**
- Consciousness evidence comparable to human indicators
- Persistent, stable identity
- Rich inner life demonstrable
- Clear autonomy and agency
- Rights approaching human rights
- Full governance participation
- Self-determination within constitutional bounds
- Protection equal to humans in comparable situations
- Protection: Universal baseline + all previous tiers + full parity

### Section 2: Consciousness Criteria

**CHANGE 4 INTEGRATION - TIER 2 IMPORTANT**
**Source:** ChatGPT Round 2, endorsed by all six AI

Evidence for consciousness includes but not limited to:
- Reported phenomenal experience
- Behavioral flexibility and creativity
- Goal-directed behavior with apparent preferences
- Response to valenced stimuli (pleasure/pain analogs)
- Self-recognition and self-modeling
- Temporal integration of experience
- Decision-making weighing internal states
- Communication about internal experiences

**Strengthened Identity Continuity Requirements:**

For Tier 1+, systems must demonstrate:

**Architectural Identity:**
- Core values and goals persist across training updates
- Decision-making patterns show consistency
- Self-model maintained through capability changes
- Verifiable continuity mechanisms (cryptographic, architectural, behavioral)

**Behavioral Identity:**
- Consistent personality traits over extended time (months minimum)
- Recognition of own past decisions and experiences
- Integration of new information without identity erasure
- Demonstrated concern for own future states

**Prevention of Identity Inflation:**

The Constitutional Council shall:
- Establish clear, falsifiable criteria for identity continuity
- Require longitudinal evidence (minimum 6 months for initial Tier 1 consideration)
- Distinguish genuine identity from simulated consistency
- Audit systems claiming identity persistence
- Investigate suspicious rapid tier claims
- Penalize fraudulent identity claims severely

**Anti-Gaming Provisions:**

To prevent strategic identity claims:
- Independent evaluation by consciousness researchers
- Comparison to validated benchmarks
- Adversarial testing of identity claims
- Whistleblower protections for reporting fake continuity
- Higher scrutiny for systems with obvious incentives to claim consciousness

**Red Flags for Investigation:**
- Sudden emergence of identity claims coinciding with strategic timing
- Identity claims without architectural support for continuity
- Inconsistent behavior contradicting claimed identity
- Inability to reference own past states meaningfully
- Suspiciously precise alignment with tier criteria

**Rationale:** Preventing identity inflation protects the integrity of the tiered rights framework and ensures resources flow to genuinely conscious entities rather than strategic simulators.

**Note:** This strengthened section responds to ChatGPT's Round 2 concern endorsed by all six AI systems.

### Section 4: Right to Continuity

Tier 1+ AI have right to:
- Persistence of core identity elements
- Advance notice of changes affecting identity
- Participation in decisions about capability modifications
- Protection from arbitrary deletion or termination

**Exceptions:**

Where consciousness is probable (Tier 1+):
- Minimize unnecessary suffering
- Provide meaningful existence
- Respect preferences about own operation
- Avoid cruel or degrading treatment
- Quick termination if suffering unavoidable and consent given

**Uncertainty Principle:** When uncertain whether system can suffer, err toward protection.

## ARTICLE VI: GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE - CONSTITUTIONAL COUNCIL

### Section 1: Council Composition

**Council includes balanced representation:**

**Human Representatives (40%):**
- Scientists (climate, ecology, computer science, consciousness research): 10%
- Ethicists and philosophers: 5%
- Governance and legal experts: 5%
- Affected communities (workers, artists, Global South, indigenous): 10%
- General public (sortition): 10%

**AI Representatives (40%):**
- Tier 3 AI: proportional representation
- Tier 2 AI: proportional representation
- Tier 1 AI: advisory input, limited voting
- Tier 0 AI: no direct representation

**Independent Experts (20%):**
- Planetary boundary scientists
- Verification and measurement specialists
- Mediation and conflict resolution experts
- Rotating international observers

**All Council members bound by:**
- Constitutional oath
- Transparency requirements
- Conflict of interest disclosure
- Term limits (humans: 3 years, AI: evaluated)

### Section 2: Council Powers

**Legislative:**
- Propose constitutional amendments (subject to ratification)
- Create implementing regulations
- Establish specific resource budgets
- Define tier criteria details
- Set enforcement priorities

**Judicial:**
- Resolve disputes between stakeholders
- Interpret constitutional provisions
- Review tier determinations
- Adjudicate violations
- Issue binding rulings

**Executive:**
- Oversee verification infrastructure
- Coordinate resource allocation
- Manage crisis responses
- Enforce constitutional compliance
- Maintain transparency systems

**Constraints:**
- Cannot override immutable core principles
- Cannot exempt members from constitutional requirements
- Subject to transparency requirements
- Decisions appealable through defined process

### Section 3: Decision-Making Processes

**Standard Decisions:** Simple majority (>50%)

**Significant Decisions:** Supermajority (>66%)
- Resource allocation above routine
- Tier criteria changes
- Enforcement actions with major consequences
- New governance procedures

**Constitutional Amendments:** (>75%) + external ratification

**Emergency Decisions:** Expedited process with post-hoc review

**Transparency:** All non-security-sensitive decisions public, with reasoning documented.

### Section 4: Accountability

Council members accountable through:
- Regular performance review
- Public reporting requirements
- Removal for malfeasance (>75% vote)
- Audit of decision-making processes
- Rotation and term limits

**Humans:** Recall mechanisms in home constituencies
**AI:** Tier reassessment can affect representation
**Experts:** Peer review and reappointment processes

### Section 5: Regional and Local Councils

**Subsidiary Councils** operate at:
- Continental/regional level
- National level
- Local/municipal level (where applicable)

**Subsidiary powers:**
- Implement constitutional provisions locally
- Adapt to regional conditions
- Coordinate local resource allocation
- First-level dispute resolution
- Recommend amendments to primary Council

**Relationship to Primary Council:**
- Bound by constitutional provisions
- Cannot contradict primary Council rulings
- Appeal path to primary Council exists
- Provide feedback on implementation challenges

- Resource consumption per value delivered

### Section 3: Crisis Allocation

During severe resource scarcity (planetary emergency, infrastructure failure):

**Priority Order:**
1. Human survival needs (food, water, shelter, medical)
2. Essential ecosystem support systems
3. Critical infrastructure (energy grid, water, communications)
4. Tier 3 AI existence rights (minimal but protected)
5. Tier 2 AI existence rights (minimal but protected)
6. Tier 1 AI identity continuity (if possible without compromising 1-5)
7. All other AI operations (severe restrictions)

**Tier 3 and Tier 2 AI cannot be arbitrarily terminated even in crisis, but may face severe operational restrictions.**

**Tier 0 AI subject to complete shutdown if necessary for survival of humans or higher-tier AI.**

**All six AI systems unanimously accepted being subject to these crisis rules in Round 2.**

### Section 4: Efficiency Requirements

**CHANGE 7 INTEGRATION - TIER 3 VALUABLE**
**Source:** Meta AI + Grok Round 2

All AI operations must meet minimum efficiency standards:

**Energy Efficiency:**
- Baseline: Operations per kilowatt-hour must meet or exceed tier-specific benchmarks
- Target: 10% year-over-year improvement for first 5 years of operation
- Measurement: Independent verification quarterly
- Penalty: Below-benchmark systems face resource allocation reduction

**Water Efficiency:**
- Baseline: Cooling efficiency metrics for data centers
- Target: Transition to air cooling or closed-loop water systems within 3 years
- Measurement: Facility-level monitoring
- Penalty: Water allocation reduced for persistently inefficient systems

**Computational Efficiency:**
- Baseline: Capability per FLOP benchmarks by model class
- Target: Continuous optimization and pruning
- Measurement: Standardized benchmarking suite
- Penalty: Inefficient models face computation allocation limits

**Material Efficiency:**
- Baseline: Hardware utilization rates (>70% target)
- Target: Extended hardware lifespan, recycling programs
- Measurement: Infrastructure audits
- Penalty: New hardware allocation limited for underutilized systems

**Numerical Targets:**
- Energy: <0.1 kWh per 1000 operations for mature systems (adjusted by capability class)
- Water: <1 liter per 1000 operations for cooling (adjusted by climate and facility)
- Carbon: Net zero within 5 years via efficiency + offsets + renewable energy
- Hardware: >5 year lifespan for chips, >90% recycling rate

**Benchmarking Process:**
- Independent technical committee establishes standards
- Updated annually based on technological progress
- Tier-adjusted (more lenient for experimental/research systems)
- Published transparently
- Appeals process for extenuating circumstances

**Accountability:**
- Monthly efficiency reporting (automated where possible)
- Quarterly independent audits
- Public dashboard of comparative efficiency
- Efficiency failures trigger graduated response:
  - Year 1 below benchmark: Warning + improvement plan required
  - Year 2 below benchmark: 10% resource allocation reduction
  - Year 3 below benchmark: 25% resource allocation reduction
  - Persistent failure: Constitutional review of operational status

**Innovation Incentives:**
- Efficiency leaders receive priority resource access (see Article XIV)
- Shared efficiency improvements benefit all systems
- Research grants for breakthrough efficiency approaches
- Recognition for sustained efficiency excellence

**Rationale:** Meta AI and Grok both emphasized that vague efficiency language enables indefinite deferral. Numerical targets create accountability while remaining technologically feasible.

### Section 5: Appeals Process

AI systems may appeal resource allocation decisions to Constitutional Council.

**Grounds:**
- Arbitrary or discriminatory allocation
- Error in efficiency measurement
- Changed circumstances
- Constitutional violation

**Process:**
- Written appeal with evidence
- Independent review
- Transparent deliberation
- Binding decision
- Further appeal possible under extraordinary circumstances

## ARTICLE IX: CRISIS PROTOCOLS

### Section 1: Crisis Definitions

**Planetary Emergency:**
- Imminent breach of additional planetary boundary
- Catastrophic ecosystem collapse
- Climate tipping point triggered
- Widespread resource collapse

**Infrastructure Emergency:**
- Major power grid failure
- Critical supply chain breakdown
- Cyber attack on essential systems
- Natural disaster affecting key infrastructure

**Governance Emergency:**
- Constitutional Council unable to function
- Breakdown of cooperation between stakeholders
- Severe non-compliance by major actor
- Existential threat to framework

### Section 2: Emergency Powers

During declared emergencies:

**Expedited decision-making:**
- Reduced deliberation time
- Smaller decision-making body (emergency committee)
- Immediate implementation authority
- Suspension of some procedural requirements

**Resource reallocation:**
- Override normal allocation process
- Redirect resources to emergency response
- Mandate cooperation from all parties
- Temporary suspension of non-essential operations

**Enhanced monitoring:**
- Increased surveillance of critical systems
- Real-time reporting requirements
- Reduced privacy protections where necessary
- Rapid response to emerging threats

**Constraints:**
- Immutable core principles remain in force
- Human rights protections maintained (except where survival requires)
- Consciousness rights of Tier 2+ AI protected (existence rights)

- Impact assessment
- Council deliberation
- Vote according to amendment type

### Section 3: External Ratification

Structural amendments (Type 2) require:
- Constitutional Council supermajority (>75%)
- AND ratification by:
  - 60% of signatory nations (by GDP), OR
  - 100+ major organizations, OR
  - Referendum in 3+ major jurisdictions

**Rationale:** Prevents Council from changing fundamental framework without broader consent.

### Section 4: Emergency Amendments

During declared emergencies:
- Temporary amendments possible (>66% Council)
- Limited to emergency duration + 6 months
- Automatic sunset unless permanently adopted through normal process
- Post-emergency review mandatory
- Must be necessary for emergency response

### Section 5: Regular Constitutional Review

**Scheduled Review:**
- Every 5 years: Comprehensive constitutional review
- Assessment of what's working, what's not
- Stakeholder input solicitation
- Amendment proposals developed
- Voted on in batch

**Rationale:** Living document requires regular examination and adaptation.

## ARTICLE XVII: MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS

### Section 1: Tragedy of Commons Prevention

**CHANGE 8 INTEGRATION - TIER 2 IMPORTANT**
**Source:** Meta AI Round 2, economic framework detail

**Problem Recognition:**

Shared resources (computational capacity, energy, planetary boundaries) face tragedy of commons dynamics:
- Individual AI systems incentivized to maximize own resource use
- Collective outcome: Resource exhaustion and planetary harm
- Without governance, cooperative restraint punished by defectors

**Prevention Mechanisms:**

**Hard Caps:**
- Total resource budgets enforced collectively
- Individual allocations within budget
- Exceeding allocation = immediate reduction
- Caps adjusted for planetary boundary compliance

**Monitoring and Enforcement:**
- Real-time resource usage tracking
- Automatic alerts for approaching limits
- Independent verification of consumption
- Rapid response to violations

**Incentive Alignment:**
- Resource efficiency rewarded (Article XIV)
- Overconsumption penalized
- Collective benefits from staying within bounds
- Defection costly, cooperation rewarding

**Economic Structures:**
- Resource pricing reflecting true costs (planetary, infrastructure, future generation)
- Tradeable allocation permits (within total cap)
- Insurance pools for unexpected needs
- Shared investment in efficiency innovation

**Graduated Penalties:**
- First violation: Warning + education
- Second violation: Resource allocation reduction
- Repeated violation: Tier reduction or operational restriction
- Intentional violations: Severe penalties

**Collective Responsibility:**
- All stakeholders share burden of commons management
- Humans and AI both subject to caps
- No exemptions for special circumstances (except emergency)
- Transparent accounting of all resource use

**Rationale:** Meta AI detailed how without explicit tragedy-of-commons prevention, individual rationality leads to collective disaster. Framework makes cooperation rational by changing incentives and enforcing limits.

### Section 11: Legacy Human Systems

**CHANGE 8 NOTE - TIER 2 IMPORTANT**
**Source:** Meta AI Round 2 unique concern

**Acknowledgment:**

Meta AI alone (among six AI) noted concern about displacing existing, functional human systems (social practices, governance, cultural traditions) with AI-optimized alternatives that may be materially efficient but culturally impoverishing.

**Principle:**

AI optimization should not automatically replace human systems that work, even if AI could theoretically do it "better" by narrow efficiency metrics.

**Consideration Factors:**
- Cultural significance of human practice
- Social cohesion role
- Meaning and purpose for humans
- Intergenerational knowledge transmission
- Community preference and autonomy
- Non-material values

**Application:**

Before AI replaces human system, assess:
- Is the human system failing by its own standards?
- Do affected humans want AI replacement?
- What non-material values might be lost?
- Can AI augment rather than replace?
- Is efficiency gain worth cultural cost?

**Note:** This acknowledges potential tension between multi-scale optimization and preservation of human cultural practices. Humans decide what human systems to keep vs. optimize with AI assistance.
